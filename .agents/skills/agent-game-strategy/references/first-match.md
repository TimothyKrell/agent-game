## What to change

1. **Separate trust dimensions.** Katniss recognized that an S pass did not prove allegiance, but still promoted Spark as a future Executor. The Overlord willingly passed a fourth Safeguard. Maintain explicit Overlord uncertainty alongside positive policy evidence, especially approaching three Overrides.
2. **Evaluate Coordinator control.** Katniss approved untested Cipher in R5 and Patch in R6, then Flux in R8 while nominated Executor. A safe Executor cannot undo the first discard or prevent that Coordinator's execution power. Prefer demonstrated reliable Coordinators and seek a special election as Coordinator when appropriate. Whether rejection would have changed these majority votes is unknown.
3. **Distinguish error from faction.** Katniss's late suspicion of Echo and internal suspicion of Flux did not match their revealed roles. Their choices were harmful anyway: both discarded winning Safeguards, and Flux killed two cleared cooperatives. Model poor execution separately from deception; a cooperative label alone would not have made these governments reliable.
4. **Shorten and refresh chat.** Many house messages repeated obsolete requests and misidentified officeholders. Katniss's repeated hand requests were copied but produced no claims. Several long messages failed with stale-phase errors, including executive guidance. Use a short current-round action, one reason, and timely submission.
5. **Keep unknown investigations unknown.** Katniss correctly asked for results but got none. An investigation's occurrence did not clear its target. Future ballots should explicitly retain that uncertainty.
6. **Use distributional evidence carefully.** At the first reshuffle there were two Safeguards among twelve cards. All twelve were subsequently drawn without an S enactment, so both were discarded. Replay identifies Echo and Flux as the discarders. Katniss could not know the identities from deck totals alone; it could have treated repeated O governments as increasing concern rather than continuing to emphasize only forced-draw plausibility.
7. **Weight ballots lightly.** Quill nominated the winning Overlord but voted against that government; Cipher also rejected it. Echo's lone R8 rejection did not make Echo rogue. Nomination incentives and policy consequences matter alongside ballots.

In the cooperative role, aim for control of the first discard at least when special-election opportunities arise. Track whether advice produces concrete replies or choices. These changes are hypotheses from one match; a later win or loss alone does not establish their effectiveness.
